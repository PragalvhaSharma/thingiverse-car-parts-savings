How this works the pipeline behind every row, and what a review click changes

One row per Thing. Each is a printable car part published on Thingiverse. The pipeline classified it, wrote a part query, searched eBay, and a strict two-stage matcher (candidate matching, then a photos-both-sides judge) kept a listing only when it shows the same physical part.

The arithmetic. A counted row saves (item price − print cost) × downloads, clamped at zero. The print cost is a real headless OrcaSlicer slice of the Thing's own STL — grams and hours at the page's recommended infill — not a flat placeholder. Prices are the item only; shipping is recorded per listing and reported as a separate sensitivity, never added into the headline.

Three buckets, never summed together. Repair is the main claim — a spare part you print instead of rebuying. Assembly-only is the subset whose part is not sold on its own, so the alternative was buying the whole assembly; it is the same repair story but a weaker comparison, so it is broken out. Upgrade is an accessory printed instead of bought — a different claim, reported alongside and never added in. Toys and decor are excluded at $0.

A broken scale is not a saving. The slicer is faithful: an STL exported at the wrong scale slices to a fraction of a gram, which would book almost the whole listing price as profit. The pipeline already refuses parts that slice too big for a consumer bed; a matching floor refuses rows whose price per printed gram is impossible — a 0.2 g spoiler, a 0.2 g cupholder. Those rows keep their price, listing and reasoning, but carry $0 and are marked excluded_scale.

Uncounted rows are not failures. About half of these parts have no equivalent single listing on eBay — they are sold only inside a complete assembly, or they are bespoke/modified pieces with no commercial equivalent. Those rows contribute $0 and the brand page shows the candidate pool the matcher refused, so a reviewer can rescue one by picking the listing that really is the same part.

Review changes the number live. Each brand page saves its decisions on the server, so every reviewer sees the same queue. Vetoing a row removes its dollars; rescuing an uncounted row adds them. apply_review.py folds those decisions back into a reviewed CSV that this page rebuilds from.
